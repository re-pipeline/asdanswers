Why oxidized glutathione matters

Oxidized glutathione (GSSG) is the disulfide form of the antioxidant glutathione (GSH). It plays a role in maintaining the redox balance within cells. GSSG can act as a reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen species (RNS) scavenger, but it can also exert inhibitory effects on certain enzymes, including SAM synthetase.

The inhibition of SAM synthetase by GSSG occurs due to the disulfide bond present in GSSG. The disulfide bond can react with and modify critical cysteine residues within the active site of SAM synthetase, thereby disrupting its enzymatic activity.

This inhibition can have significant consequences since SAM is involved in numerous methylation reactions that are essential for cellular processes, including DNA and protein methylation, neurotransmitter synthesis, and detoxification reactions. Impairment of SAM synthetase activity can disrupt these processes and have a broad impact on cellular function.
